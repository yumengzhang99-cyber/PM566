Question 2

mt_samples |> 
  unnest_tokens(token, transcription) |>
  count(token, sort=TRUE) |> 
  top_n(20,n) |>
  ggplot(aes(n, fct_reorder(token, n))) +
  geom_col()

The top 20 words are almost all function words like “the,” “and,” “was,” and “of.” The only content words that stand out are “patient” and “right”.” This makes sense for narrative clinical notes, but the plot tells us very little about what the notes are about.

Question 3

mt_samples |> 
  unnest_tokens(token, transcription) |>
  anti_join(stop_words, by = c("token"="word")) |> 
  filter(!grepl("^[0-9.]+$", token)) |>   #remove all token of numbers include decimals.
  count(token, sort=TRUE) |> 
  top_n(20,n) |>
  ggplot(aes(n, fct_reorder(token, n))) +
  geom_col()

After removing stop words and numbers, the list becomes informative: patient, right, left, history, normal, procedure, placed, pain, blood, and anesthesia. These point to surgical activity, physical exams, and patient complaints. It gives a much clearer picture of the text than Question 2 did.

Question 7

mt_samples |>
  unnest_tokens(token, transcription) |>
  filter(token == "smoke") |>
  count(medical_specialty, sort = TRUE) |>
  ggplot(aes(n, fct_reorder(medical_specialty, n))) +
  geom_col() +
  labs(x = "Count of 'smoke'", y = "Medical Specialty")

The word “smoke” shows up most often in Consult - History and Phy. notes (about 72 times). These notes usually include a social history, which is probably why. General Medicine (about 21), Cardiovascular / Pulmonary (about 20), and Emergency Room Reports (about 16) come next. Most other specialties have 10 or fewer mentions.

This chart shows raw counts, so specialties with more notes will naturally show higher numbers.

totals <- mt_samples |>
  count(medical_specialty, name = "total")

mt_samples |>
  mutate(id = row_number()) |>
  unnest_tokens(token, transcription) |>
  filter(token == "smoke") |>
  distinct(id, medical_specialty) |>
  count(medical_specialty, name = "smoke_notes") |>
  left_join(totals, by = "medical_specialty") |>
  mutate(pct = smoke_notes / total * 100) |>
  arrange(desc(pct)) |>
  ggplot(aes(pct, fct_reorder(medical_specialty, pct))) +
  geom_col() +
  labs(x = "% of notes mentioning 'smoke'", y = "Medical Specialty")

When we look at the share of notes that mention “smoke,” the ranking changes a lot. Chiropractic and Emergency Room Reports are highest, at about 21% each, followed by IME-QME-Work Comp (about 19%) and Hospice - Palliative Care (about 17%). Consult - History and Phy. is about 14%, which is much lower than its raw count suggested. Surgery is near 0%.
